## 1. Three findings that change the plan

Read these before the restructure proposal. Two of them are defects, and one of
them probably explains the paper results.

### 1.1 Missing indicator data scores as *maximum confidence*

`apps/api/app/services/paper_strategy.py`, `_score()`. Four of the five scoring
sections award **full marks when their input is absent**, while awarding **zero**
when the input is present and unfavourable:

| Section | Max | Input missing | Input present but unfavourable |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| VWAP alignment | 20 | **20** | 0 (wrong side of VWAP) |
| Volume / RVOL | 20 | **20** | 0 (below the multiple) |
| NIFTY regime | 10 | **10** | 0 (regime disagrees) |
| Relative strength | 10 | **10** | 0 (wrong direction) |

An instrument with **no** VWAP, RVOL, regime or relative-strength data collects
60 of the 100 points for free, and needs only 20 of the remaining 40 (breakout +
EMA) to clear the `minimum_score: 80` threshold.

Why this matters more than it looks: the RVOL baseline needs
`RVOL_BASELINE_SESSIONS=10` sessions of history, and the instance holds roughly
**eight** sessions of 1-minute data. So RVOL was plausibly absent — and silently
scoring 20/20 — for much or all of the paper sample whose results we have been
analysing. The same reasoning applies to relative strength and regime, both of
which need NIFTY history.

I cannot prove from here how often it fired without querying your
`paper_signals.score_breakdown` rows, and that is the first query I would run.
But the code is unambiguous, and "absent evidence scores as strong evidence" is
a defect regardless of frequency.

**Proposed rule:** a required input that is missing or stale **blocks** the
signal. An optional input that is missing scores **zero**, never full. Which
inputs are required becomes a per-strategy setting.

### 1.2 The daily risk budget silently caps you at two trades

`DEFAULT_TRADING_CONTROLS` in `apps/api/app/api/routes/settings.py`:

```
account_capital              10,000
risk_per_trade_percent          1.0   ->  ₹100 planned risk per trade
maximum_daily_risk_percent      2.0   ->  ₹200 daily risk budget
maximum_signals                   4
maximum_open_positions            4
daily_profit_target             0.0   (disabled)
daily_loss_limit                0.0   (disabled)
```

₹200 ÷ ₹100 = **2 entries**. The 3rd and 4th are refused with "Daily paper-risk
allocation limit reached" no matter what `maximum_signals` says. This is the
hidden 2% limit you suspected. Note these are the *defaults*; your NAS profile
(`scripts/apply-trading-controls.sh`) sets 5.5% / loss 1000 / profit 2000 /
signals 3 / positions 1, which is self-consistent — but any fresh install or
missing settings row falls back to the contradictory set above.

Also latent: `maximum_open_exposure_percent: 100` is multiplied by leverage, so
100 means ₹50,000 of exposure, not ₹10,000. The label reads as a percent of
capital and is not.

### 1.3 "Four trades a day" currently counts signals, not fills

`apps/api/app/services/scanner_orchestration.py:127` counts rows in
`paper_signals` where status is not `PAPER_RISK_REJECTED`. Your specification is
an entry's **first fill**. These differ whenever a signal is accepted by risk but
its entry order never fills, or is cancelled — the budget is consumed by a trade
that never happened.

**Proposed rule, as you specified it:** the counter increments when an ENTRY
order records its first fill; later partial fills of the same entry increment
nothing; signals, evaluations, duplicates and zero-fill rejections increment
nothing. Account-wide across strategies and brokers, reconciled from
`paper_fills` / `live_order_submissions` on restart rather than held in memory.

## 5. Upstox history APIs — researched, and one finding that shapes the design

Verified against the current developer reference:

| Purpose | Endpoint | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Today's orders | `GET /v2/order/retrieve-all` | full order records incl. `tag`; **today only** |
| Positions | `GET /v2/portfolio/short-term-positions` | `quantity`, `pnl`, `realised`, `unrealised` |
| Historical P&L | `GET /v2/trade/profit-loss/data` | matched buy/sell pairs per scrip: `isin`, `scrip_name`, `quantity`, `buy_date`, `buy_average`, `sell_date`, `sell_average`, `buy_amount`, `sell_amount`. Requires `segment` + `financial_year` ("2526"), paged |
| Charges | `GET /v2/trade/profit-loss/charges` | `charges_breakdown`: `brokerage`, `taxes.gst`, `taxes.stt`, `taxes.stamp_duty`, `charges.transaction`, `charges.sebi_turnover` |

**The finding: charges are returned aggregated for a date range, not per trade.**
There is no per-trade charge figure available from the broker at all.

Consequences for the History screen, which I want agreed before I build it:

1. Per-trade itemised costs are **always locally estimated**. `ESTIMATED CHARGES`
   is therefore the *normal* steady state for a trade row, not an error.
2. Reconciliation of charges happens at **date-range level**: sum our estimates
   for the period, compare against the broker aggregate, show both and the
   difference. Never overwrite.
3. The broker P&L report carries **no order ids** and is matched-pair shaped, so
   it cannot be joined to our rows by identity — only by scrip, date and quantity.
   That is a fuzzy match, and I will show it as such rather than implying certainty.
4. `retrieve-all` is today-only, which confirms your instinct: **local event
   persistence is the archive.** The broker is a comparison source.
